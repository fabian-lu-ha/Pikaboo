# Vision — Brand Autopilot

## The world we're building for

Every early-stage brand is invisible in the answer-engine era.

When someone asks ChatGPT *"best CRM for startups,"* the answer is Salesforce or HubSpot — never Attio. When they ask *"best EV in 2026,"* it's Tesla — never BYD. When they ask *"phone with the cleanest software,"* it's Apple — never Nothing. The big incumbents are baked into the training data. Newcomers don't have the budget for the agency-grade content machine it takes to muscle their way into LLM answers, comparison listicles, Reddit threads, YouTube reviews, and TikTok feeds — across every channel that now feeds the AI search engines.

Modern marketing is a full-time team. Most early-stage brands can't afford one. So they ship a product, get crickets in AI search, and watch the incumbents extend their lead.

## What we're building

An AI marketing co-founder.

Not a tool. A colleague. The thing every early-stage brand wishes they could afford: a senior operator who knows the brand inside out, watches the competitive field 24/7, knows what actually works in the answer-engine era, generates content the brand actually wants to ship, and never sleeps.

You onboard once. The agent learns your brand. Then it just runs your weekly marketing.

## How it feels to use

**Onboarding feels like introducing a new hire to your company.** You give it the basics — what you do, who you serve, the link to your site. Optionally you connect your Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, X — the agent reads your last 30 posts and learns how you talk. You list your top competitors, or it suggests them. You point to a couple of brands whose marketing you admire. Five minutes. Now it knows you.

**The home screen is a conversation.** A big chat box, Lovable-style, sits in the middle. Above it, a strip of "what to do next" cards — three to five suggestions the agent has prepared since you last looked. To the right, a live ticker of competitor moves: who surged in AI search this week, who slipped, what new sources started getting cited for them. Below, every campaign you've ever run, ready to revisit or replay.

Open the chat and ask anything:

> *"What should I post this week?"*
> *"Make me a launch campaign for the feature we just shipped."*
> *"Counter Salesforce's surge on startup CRM."*
> *"Write me a YouTube Shorts series introducing the product."*

The agent goes to work in front of you. It pulls your visibility data. It looks at what's currently winning in your space. It drafts content in your voice. It generates visuals that look like you, not generic AI slop. It tells you, before you even approve anything, how much each piece is predicted to move your AI-search visibility — and shows the math, grounded in published research, not vibes.

You review, swipe to approve or reject, edit if you want. The agent packages everything into a campaign bundle and either hands it to a junior to ship or — when you trust it — publishes directly.

## A Monday morning with Brand Autopilot

Setting the scene. You run a 12-person B2B SaaS — let's call it *Klarro*. You're the founder. It's Monday, 9:14 AM. Two coffees in. You open the laptop.

Above the chat box, three suggestion cards have been waiting since you closed the app Friday:

- *"Salesforce surged +14% on 'CRM for Series A startups' over the weekend. Counter-move?"*
- *"You shipped Bulk-Edit Friday afternoon. Launch campaign ready to draft."*
- *"New listicle 'Best CRMs in 2026' on TechCrunch — Salesforce mentioned, you weren't. Outreach draft?"*

The competitor radar on the right shows HubSpot dropped a comparison post Friday. Three new Reddit threads cited Salesforce in the last 48 hours. Pipedrive's visibility dipped 4%.

You click the second card. The chat opens with a draft: *"Launch campaign for Bulk-Edit. Generate."*

The agent starts working in front of you. You watch the steps stream in:

- Pulls the Bulk-Edit Linear ticket and the changelog you wrote Friday.
- Calls Peec, identifies five prompts where the launch could land — *"best CRM for ops teams,"* *"CRM bulk operations,"* *"Salesforce alternatives for fast-moving teams,"* *"Klarro vs HubSpot,"* *"CRM with bulk editing."*
- Drafts the announcement blog post — listicle structure, three quotation blocks pulled from your founder updates, two stat callouts from your usage data, schema markup.
- Generates a hero image: your product on a desk in your brand's mint-and-charcoal palette. Looks like you, not like Midjourney.
- Drafts five social posts — LinkedIn for the founder, X thread for the engineering team, Instagram caption with the hero, TikTok hook with shot list, Reddit answer for r/startups.
- Predicts the lift: *"+18% visibility on the targeted prompts. Three of the five posts target source domains LLMs already cite for HubSpot."*

9:21 AM. You swipe through the drafts. Approve four. Send the LinkedIn post back with *"make it less press-release-y."* Two minutes later it's redrafted in your voice.

9:24 AM. You hit Export. The campaign bundle drops into Slack for your junior to ship today. Or — if you trust it — Auto-Publish, and it's all live by 11.

You close the laptop. That was your marketing department for the morning.

## What it knows about you — and how it keeps learning

It knows you, and it keeps learning. The initial profile is just a starting point — onboarding gives the agent your description, your site, your products, the social posts you fed it. But the model of *you* sharpens every time you approve a draft, reject one, or send something back with a tweak. Six months in, the agent knows your voice better than any junior hire ever could — because it has seen 200 of your approved drafts and 100 of your *"no, less press-release-y"* notes, and trained on every one.

It learns from your competitors too. The agent studies the brands you compete with continuously — which formats they're investing in, which posts get traction, which sources start citing them, what they tried that flopped. Not to copy. To know the shape of the field and where the real openings are.

It learns from the styles you admire. During onboarding you point to two or three brands whose marketing you respect — maybe Linear's restraint, Vercel's polish, Notion's clarity. The agent blends elements of those tastes into your voice without losing what makes you, you. As you flag more brands over time, the taste graph deepens.

It knows the competitive field in real time. Every top competitor is watched continuously. The moment one of them surges, the agent flags it and proposes a counter-move.

It knows what's actually working. The agent operates on top of Peec AI — the data layer that tells us, for any prompt across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, Grok, Copilot, and Google AI Overviews, who's mentioned, who's not, and which sources are being cited. We're not guessing. We're not vibing. We're running marketing on the data that incumbents already pay enterprise prices to access.

It knows what your team is shipping. Wire up your kanban — Linear, Notion, GitHub Projects — and the agent sees the features you just released. The launch campaign is sitting on your home screen the morning after the merge.

And it knows what to make. Across every format you'd ever ship:

- **Articles and blog posts** structured the way LLMs actually cite (listicle and comparison formats, with the quotations, statistics, and citations that the GEO research shows lift visibility 30–40%)
- **Social posts** for LinkedIn, X, Instagram, TikTok, Reddit — each tuned to the platform's idiom
- **Hero images and social cards** that look like you, not like generic AI slop, via brand-reference conditioning
- **Comparison pages** and **listicles** — the formats LLMs cite the most
- **YouTube Shorts series** with consistent visual themes and recurring characters
- **TikTok and Reels scripts** with shot lists
- **Full landing pages**
- **Email sequences**
- **Press releases**
- **Multi-week content strategies** — not just one post, the whole plan

Every campaign comes with a number. *"Adding three quotation blocks, two statistic callouts, and a structured-data block on this comparison page → predicted +63% visibility on the prompt 'best CRM for startups,' based on published GEO research."* You trust because the math is visible.

## What a campaign bundle actually looks like

Concrete, so the capability isn't vague. One run for *Klarro* launching Bulk-Edit might produce:

**Blog post**
> *"Why we replaced Salesforce with Klarro at 12 employees (and saved 6 weeks of setup)"*
> 800-word listicle. Three quotation blocks pulled from your last three founder updates. Two statistic callouts ("47% setup time reduction"). schema.org `Article` + `Organization` markup. Internal links to the comparison page below.

**LinkedIn post (founder voice)**
> *"Bulk-Edit shipped. We took a feature Salesforce charges $150/seat for and gave it to every Klarro team. Here's why bulk operations were our most-requested feature, and what it took to build them right..."*
> 280 words. CTA links to the announcement post.

**X thread (engineering voice)**
> 8 tweets. Code-flavored. Why bulk operations are deceptively hard ("you have to pick: optimistic UI with rollback, or sync writes that block on N records — we tried both"). Ends on a screenshot of a bulk-edit running in 4 seconds against 200 contacts.

**Instagram caption + hero image**
> *"Bulk-Edit. Now in your sidebar. 🟢"*
> Hero: product shot in a workspace, mint-and-charcoal palette, one screenshot of the new feature visible. Brand-consistent because the agent conditioned the generation on your logo and previous product shots.

**TikTok script (15-second hook)**
> Beat 1 (0–3s): Founder face to camera — *"I just spent six weeks rebuilding what Salesforce charges $150/seat for."*
> Beat 2 (3–8s): Screen recording — bulk-editing 200 contacts in 4 seconds.
> Beat 3 (8–12s): Cut to founder — *"And we gave it to every Klarro user."*
> Beat 4 (12–15s): Logo + handle.

**Reddit answer (r/startups)**
> Reply to the active thread *"Best CRM for early-stage that won't bankrupt us at 50 seats?"*. 220 words. Mentions Klarro alongside three competitors honestly. Lists tradeoffs. No pitch language. The agent recognized this thread is the kind LLMs actually cite (established, organic, multiple legitimate replies).

**Comparison page snippet**
> *"Klarro vs Salesforce for startups under 50"* — side-by-side table, eight dimensions, schema.org `Product` + `sameAs` markup so LLMs pull it cleanly.

**YouTube Shorts series concept**
> Five-episode arc, *"How we built ___."* Recurring host (you), recurring 2-second intro animation, recurring sign-off. Episode 1 is Bulk-Edit (this campaign). Episodes 2–5 lined up from the Linear backlog, ready to draft when those features ship.

**Distribution recipe** (CSV ready for your junior to paste)
> 8 rows. Each: which channel, which time, which copy, which link. The whole launch sequenced.

**Predicted lift**
> *"+18% visibility on the five targeted prompts over 30 days. Confidence: medium. Math grounded in: KDD 2024 GEO paper (quotation lift +42.6%, statistic lift +32.8%) + Peec source-overlap analysis (3 of 5 target domains already cited for HubSpot)."*

That's one campaign. The agent runs as many of these per week as you want.

## When you trust it: autonomous mode

The default is collaborative — the agent proposes, you approve. But the long arc of the product is different. The more the agent learns your voice, the more it earns the right to ship without asking.

First it earns autonomy on the small stuff — a tweet promoting your latest blog post, a Reddit answer that scores 95% voice-fit, an Instagram caption on a hero you already approved. Auto-publish. Daily digest in the morning showing what went out. Then it earns autonomy on whole launch campaigns once you've approved enough variants of the same recipe. Eventually the agent runs your marketing while you're focused on the product.

Triggers come from outside the chat box too. A pull request lands in Linear marked *ship-ready* — the launch campaign drafts itself overnight, sitting on your desk by 9 AM. A competitor surges 12% on a target prompt — a counter-move ships within the hour. A comparison listicle gets a new ChatGPT citation — a follow-up piece fires automatically. A new feature flag flips to GA in your codebase — the announcement post auto-publishes.

Marketing as a function with a heartbeat, not a thing you do. Open the app once a week to see what shipped.

## The library that compounds

Every campaign that works gets saved as a recipe. Click it next quarter, swap the parameters, run it again. The agent's best moves become institutional knowledge. The library is searchable, branchable, replayable. *"Re-run the Q3 launch playbook for the new product."* Done.

Memory is the moat. The longer you use it, the more it knows about what works for *your* brand specifically — not the average B2B SaaS, *yours*.
