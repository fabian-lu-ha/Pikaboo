# Channel Playbook — Brand Autopilot

*Research-backed spec for per-channel drafts + per-channel images. Source of truth for the agent loop refactor.*

---

## 1. The frame

**One campaign, multiple channels, each post tailored to the channel's idiom and adapted to the brand's voice/visual style.**

The single biggest amateur tell in 2026 is identical content fanned out to every network. As Hootsuite, Planable and Sprout all observe in their 2026 cross-posting guides, "posting the exact same message to every network without editing it for the specific requirements and audience demands of those networks can make you look amateur or robotic at best and untrustworthy at worst" ([Hootsuite, 2026](https://blog.hootsuite.com/cross-promote-social-media/); [Planable, 2026](https://planable.io/blog/cross-posting-social-media/); [Sprout Social, 2026](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/data/2026-social-media-content-strategy-report/)). A horizontal video that lands on Facebook looks broken on TikTok; a 1,800-character LinkedIn essay reads as a wall-of-text on X.

The 2026 principle, repeated across Buffer, Hootsuite, Sprout, and Sendible: **one core idea, dressed differently per channel.** Brand voice = the stable personality (consistent across channels). Tone = how that voice modulates for context. Visual identity = a stable system whose *recipe* changes per channel: LinkedIn gets restraint and typography; Instagram gets saturation and lifestyle; X gets a single punchy idea. The goal is *recognisably different but unmistakably the same brand.* ([Sprout Seeds — Voice and tone](https://seeds.sproutsocial.com/writing/voice-and-tone/); [StoryChief — Adapting brand voice](https://storychief.io/blog/how-i-adapt-brand-voice-for-linkedin-instagram-and-newsletters)).

This is what Brand Autopilot must produce. Not a generic "social post + blog + hero image". A *coordinated set* of channel-native artefacts.

## 2. Per-channel deep-dive

### LinkedIn (feed post)

**Text**
- **Length.** 1,300–1,900 characters is the engagement sweet spot in 2026; ~210 chars sit above the "see more" fold (140 on mobile) and act as the headline ([connectsafely.ai](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/ideal-linkedin-post-length-engagement-guide-2026); [TypeCount LI 2026](https://typecount.com/blog/linkedin-character-limit); [PowerIn 2026 guide](https://www.powerin.io/blog/linkedin-post-character-limit)). For Brand Autopilot we draft **130–220 words ≈ 800–1,400 chars** — the upper-quartile of the engagement curve without bloat.
- **Idiom.** Hook in line 1 (provocative question, surprising stat, or story teaser). Body in short 1-2 line paragraphs separated by blank lines. End with a concrete CTA + soft sign-off. No "I'm excited to announce" openers.
- **Hashtags.** 3–5, **at the end**, never scattered ([connectsafely.ai LI hashtags](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/linkedin-hashtags); [distribution.ai 2026](https://www.distribution.ai/blog/linkedin-hashtags)). 6+ tanks reach.
- **Links.** External links cost ~30–50% reach. If linking, drop in first comment ("Link below ↓").

**Best-performing patterns 2026**
- **The carousel** (8–12 slides, PDF doc post) is one of the highest-engagement post types — high dwell time, every swipe = engagement signal ([wavegen.ai LinkedIn carousel](https://wavegen.ai/linkedin-carousel-size); [carouselmaker.co](https://carouselmaker.co/en/blog/linkedin-carousel-size-dimensions-guide)).
- **Personal lesson** ("here's what we learned shipping X"), with a quoted line or stat callout.
- **Bullet teardowns** (numbered list above, expanded paragraph below).

**Image**
- Aspect **1.91:1**, dimensions **1200×627** (link-preview / banner format), JPEG ≤5 MB.
- *What works*: editorial banner — restrained palette, strong typographic hierarchy, one focal subject + caption-safe negative space. Looks like a magazine cover, not an ad.
- *What doesn't*: stock corporate handshakes, AI-generated boardroom scenes, rendered "Get started" CTA buttons, generic bar-chart-on-laptop.

**Don'ts (AI-slop tells)**
- Stock corporate handshake or generic open-plan office — long-flagged dead in 2026 corporate comms ([Capturely 2026](https://capturely.com/companies-moving-away-from-ai-headshots/)).
- AI infographics with garbled words / fake data callouts ([colecandoo, 2026](https://colecandoo.com/2026/02/07/stop-making-ai-slop-for-social-media-clout/)).
- "Look how AI I am" 3D chrome aesthetic.

### Instagram (feed post)

**Text**
- **Caption.** 2,200-char ceiling, but only the **first 125 chars** show before "more". Best practice: 60–110 words ≈ 400–700 chars, with a 1-line hook front-loaded ([typecount IG 2026](https://typecount.com/blog/instagram-caption-character-limit); [lamplightcreatives, 2026](https://lamplightcreatives.com/captions-vs-hashtags-instagram-2026/)).
- **Idiom.** Punchy hook → 2–3 short paragraphs separated by line breaks (avoid wall-of-text on phone) → soft CTA ("save for later", "tag someone who…").
- **Hashtags.** 3–5 high-relevance tags. Meta deprioritised hashtags in 2026 — keyword-rich captions outperform tag soup. Place hashtags in the **first comment**, not the caption ([Snappa IG hashtags 2026](https://snappa.com/blog/instagram-hashtags/); [lamplightcreatives 2026](https://lamplightcreatives.com/captions-vs-hashtags-instagram-2026/)).
- **Links.** No clickable links in caption — drive to bio.

**Best-performing patterns 2026**
- **Portrait 4:5 carousel (1080×1350)** — takes ~25% more screen than square; consistently highest saves/shares ([wavegen.ai IG carousel](https://wavegen.ai/instagram-carousel-size)). Slide 1 = bold-text hook.
- **Lifestyle / product-in-context** square shots beat clean studio cutouts.
- **Selfie-style talking-head reel** with burned caption.

**Image**
- Feed: **1:1**, **1080×1080**, JPEG/PNG. Carousel: **4:5**, **1080×1350** (preferred).
- *What works*: warm, saturated, lifestyle, product-in-hand, on-set, in-place. Off-centre composition, room for caption-overlay if any.
- *What doesn't*: rendered UI buttons, neon AI gradients, perfectly symmetric "logo on background" graphic-design tile (looks like a 2018 ad).

**Don'ts (AI-slop tells)**
- A "post tile" with rendered text-as-button ("LEARN MORE") — instantly reads as AI.
- 6+ subjects in a fake group photo (Nano Banana Pro caps consistency at 5 — beyond that = morphing).
- Watermarked re-uploads from TikTok — Meta deprioritises these ([Hootsuite cross-post 2026](https://blog.hootsuite.com/cross-promote-social-media/)).

### Instagram (Story / Reel cover)

**Text**
- Burnt-in caption ≤30 chars/line, ≥42 px font, 1.3× line spacing for readability.

**Image**
- **9:16, 1080×1920, JPEG/PNG.** Top ~250 px and bottom ~250–280 px are UI-occluded. **Caption-safe zone = centre 1080×1390 (≈ y:250–1640).** Add 40–60 px L/R padding ([wavegen IG Story 2026](https://wavegen.ai/instagram-story-size); [zeely.ai 2026](https://zeely.ai/blog/master-instagram-safe-zones/); [overvisual safe-zone tool](https://www.overvisual.com/tools/instagram-story-safe-zone)).
- *What works*: focal subject vertically centred, ample negative space top + bottom, high-contrast burned-in caption inside the safe zone.
- *What doesn't*: text near the edges, key logo at the top (covered by username/avatar), CTA at the very bottom (covered by reply bar).

**Don'ts (AI-slop tells)**
- A horizontal image padded with blurred edges to fill 9:16 (looks like cross-post; Meta penalises).
- Important text outside the centre safe zone.

### X (formerly Twitter)

**Text**
- **≤280 chars** (Premium goes higher but treat 280 as the ceiling — quoted/embedded as 280) ([advancedcharactercounter X 2026](https://advancedcharactercounter.com/twitter-x-character-limit-complete-2026-guide-for-posts-bio-dms-and-more/)).
- **Idiom.** One distilled thought. A statistic, a contrarian take, a quotable line. No threads in v1.
- **Hashtags.** 0–1, only if it's a known community tag.
- **Links.** External links cost reach. If linking, drop the link in a reply.

**Best-performing patterns 2026**
- **Punchy declarative + image** ("we shipped X. Here's what changed.") with a 16:9 hero.
- **Short data callout** ("82% of [audience] hate [problem]. We built [thing].").
- **Quoted line** from a longer post elsewhere.

**Image**
- **16:9, 1200×675 (or 1600×900 for crisper desktop), JPEG/PNG/WebP, ≤5 MB** ([HeyOrca X 2026](https://www.heyorca.com/blog/x-twitter-media-specs-best-practices-2026); [Tweet Archivist 2026](https://www.tweetarchivist.com/twitter-image-size-guide); [postfa.st X 2026](https://postfa.st/sizes/x/posts)). Up to 4 images per post.
- *What works*: a single product shot, a single chart, a single statement card. Bold, readable on small screens, plenty of negative space.
- *What doesn't*: full-screenshot-of-an-article (lazy), text crammed corner-to-corner.

**Don'ts (AI-slop tells)**
- A "tile" with the tweet's own text rendered into the image.
- Generic AI scenery used as wallpaper behind a quote.

### TikTok

**Text (script + caption)**
- **Script hook in first 2–3 seconds** decides 80% of outcomes — 65% of viewers scroll in <3 s ([flowcree TikTok script](https://www.flowcree.com/en/blog/viral-tiktok-script); [opus.pro Anatomy of viral TikTok 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026); [sendshort.ai TikTok hooks](https://sendshort.ai/guides/tiktok-hooks/)).
- **Caption** = extension of hook, not description. ≤150 chars, curiosity-driven, no hashtag spam. 2–3 hashtags max.
- Conversational, high-energy register (~20% above normal speech).

**Best-performing patterns 2026**
- **Question hook**: "Are you still doing X wrong?" / "Here's why nobody buys your Y."
- **Transformation reveal** in first frame.
- **Bold on-screen caption** echoing (not duplicating) the voiceover.

**Image (cover)**
- **9:16, 1080×1920, JPEG/PNG.** On the profile grid TikTok crops to ~3:4 — keep key text/face centred vertically.
- *What works*: hook text in big bright sans-serif, single focal face/product, high contrast.
- *What doesn't*: moody / dark covers (lose against the feed); thin elegant typography (illegible at thumbnail size).

**Don'ts (AI-slop tells)**
- Title text cropped by the profile-grid 3:4 crop.
- AI-rendered "person" pretending to be a creator.
- Watermarked TikTok cover re-uploaded to other platforms.

### Blog (long-form on the brand's own site)

**Text**
- 350–450 words for a tight listicle / 1,200–1,800 for a feature. Brand Autopilot defaults to **350–450 words**.
- Listicle structure: hook → 3–5 numbered/bulleted points → quotation block → statistic callout → conclusion + CTA. Quote blocks + numeric callouts boost AI-search (GEO) visibility.
- Markdown.

**Image (header)**
- **16:9, 1600×900, JPEG/PNG.** Industry standard for editorial headers ([img-sharing 2026](https://www.img-sharing.com/blog/social-media-image-sizes-2026); [tiny-img website image 2026](https://tiny-img.com/blog/best-image-size-for-website/)).
- *What works*: editorial illustration or photograph — supports article hierarchy without competing with the title; soft enough that an H1 on top is still readable.
- *What doesn't*: a title-card with the article title rendered into the image (the H1 is already on the page).

**Don'ts (AI-slop tells)**
- "AI-generated abstract concept of [topic]" — dated.
- Stock-photo of a laptop on a desk.

---

### Newsletter

**Text**
- **100–249 words** = highest CTR in 2026 ([automateed newsletter length 2026](https://www.automateed.com/how-long-should-newsletters-be); [whop newsletter stats 2026](https://whop.com/blog/newsletter-statistics/)). Brand Autopilot defaults to **≤500 words**.
- **Register: intimate "you and me".** Personality + hot takes outperform corporate summaries; a 10k engaged list beats a 100k passive one.
- Format for scanning: short paragraphs, one-line callouts, single primary link.

**Image (hero)**
- **16:9, max 600–650 px wide on render** (so the source asset can be 1200×675 or 1600×900, scaled). PNG for logos, JPEG for photos. Keep file ≤200 KB ([moosend email sizes 2026](https://moosend.com/blog/email-sizes/); [mailmodo email image 2026](https://www.mailmodo.com/guides/email-image-size/)).
- *What works*: warm, editorial; feels like the inside of a curated weekly.
- *What doesn't*: heavy gradients (mail clients render them oddly); embedded webfonts (often blocked).

**Don'ts (AI-slop tells)**
- Generic "newsletter masthead" template look.

---

### YouTube (Short + standard)

**Text (titles, descriptions)**
- Title ≤60 chars, hook-driven.

**Image (thumbnail)**
- **Standard: 16:9, 1280×720, JPEG/PNG, ≤2 MB.**
- **Shorts: source video is 9:16 (1080×1920) but custom thumbnails are still 16:9 1280×720.** ([socialrails YT thumbnail 2026](https://socialrails.com/blog/youtube-thumbnail-size-dimensions-guide); [vidiq YT thumbnail 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-size-measurements/); [growthos Shorts thumbnail](https://growthos.in/blog/youtube-shorts-thumbnails-size)).
- **Shorts with custom thumbnails containing readable text had 85% higher CTR** than auto-generated frames.
- *What works*: clear focal subject (face or product) + 3–6 word text overlay + high-contrast colour.
- *What doesn't*: thin-typography titles, mid-tone backgrounds, more than 6 words of overlay text.

**Don'ts (AI-slop tells)**
- "MrBeast face" parody arrows + clickbait shock — burned out.
- Tiny illegible text ("AI generated thumbnail").

## 3. Style adaptation per channel

The brand's captured `style_profile` is a *recipe* that mixes differently per channel. The brand should look unmistakably itself in every output, but the channel reshapes the dial.

| Channel | Palette use | Composition | Treatment | Typography | Mood dial |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | Restrained — primary + 1 accent only | Editorial, banner-like, asymmetric, room for headline overlay | More photographic / documentary | Strong hierarchy; large H1 if any text | 80% of brand mood, +20% professional |
| **IG feed (1:1)** | Saturated, full palette in play | Centred or rule-of-thirds product/lifestyle | Lifestyle, in-context, in-hand | Minimal — usually no text | 100% brand mood, warmer |
| **IG story (9:16)** | Brand primary + bg colour from palette_roles | Vertical, focal subject in centre safe-zone, ample top/bottom space | Photo or graphic | Burned-in caption ≥42 px in safe zone | 100% brand mood |
| **X (16:9)** | Single-colour bg + product, or 2-tone | Minimal, single statement, generous negative space | Product-cutout or chart | Optional one-line headline | 100% brand mood, distilled |
| **TikTok cover (9:16)** | Brightest, highest-contrast slice of palette | Vertical, focal subject + hook text, key elements centred (3:4 crop survives) | Often illustrated or punchy graphic | Big bold sans-serif overlay | 110% brand mood, energy maxed |
| **Blog header (16:9)** | Full palette as illustration/photograph | Editorial, supports article hierarchy, soft so H1 reads on top | Illustration or photograph, no embedded title | None | 100% brand mood, calm |
| **Newsletter hero (16:9)** | Warm, intimate, often softer than feed | Editorial, scannable | Photographic or warm illustration | None | 90% brand mood, intimate |

**Rule that ties it together.** Every channel shares: the brand's **primary + 1 accent colour**, the **distinctive_marks** (e.g. "circular product crop", "hand-drawn underline"), and the **photographic_vs_illustrated** treatment. That's what makes them all read as the same brand. What varies: aspect, density, saturation, presence/absence of overlaid type, and how the focal subject is composed for that channel's UI.

## 4. Voice adaptation per channel

Brand voice is the stable personality. Tone shifts per platform. Use the captured `voice_profile` (tone, recurring_phrases, do[], dont[], voice_excerpt) as the constant and modulate via these per-channel notes:

| Channel | Voice mix | Length | Modulation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | 80% brand voice, +20% polish | 130–220 words | Slightly more formal sentence structure. Keep recurring_phrases. Swap slang for crisp business prose. Story arc with one personal beat. |
| **Instagram caption** | 100% brand voice | 60–110 words | More emotional, more direct address ("you"), more line breaks. Recurring phrases up-weighted. |
| **X tweet** | 100% brand voice, distilled | 12–50 words | Single thought. Quotable. One stat or sharp claim. Cut every adjective that isn't doing work. |
| **TikTok script** | 100% brand voice, +20% energy | 50–120 spoken words | Conversational. Hook in first 2 seconds. Question / contrarian opener. One concrete payoff. |
| **Blog body** | 100% brand voice, fully expanded | 350–450 words | Listicle structure. Quote block + stat callout (boosts GEO). Recurring phrases naturally placed. |
| **Newsletter** | 90% brand voice, +10% intimacy | ≤500 words | "You and me" register. Personal hot take. One primary CTA. Subject-line hook. |

**Prompt-engineering note (universal).** Each draft prompt must inject:
1. The full `voice_profile` block (tone, recurring_phrases, do, dont, voice_excerpt).
2. The channel's **idiom_notes** (one or two sentences of channel-specific guidance pulled from the registry).
3. The channel's **voice_adaptation_note** (the mix from the table above).
4. The channel's **anti_patterns** (what *not* to do — explicit "AI-slop on this channel looks like X").
5. The user's request, last, so it's the freshest context.

This stack consistently beats "generic copywriter system prompt + user request".

## 5. Use of brand assets in image gen

When the brand has uploaded product photos / assets, those assets should anchor the image — they tell the model "make output look like the brand's actual photography, not generic AI-art".

**Mechanism.** Nano Banana Pro (`gemini-3-pro-image-preview`) accepts up to **14 reference images** in a single request as additional `Part.from_bytes(...)` entries in the `contents` array; up to 6 high-fidelity object refs and up to 5 character-consistency refs ([Google AI image generation docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation); [DeepMind Gemini 3 Pro Image](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-image/pro/); [Together AI Nano Banana Pro](https://www.together.ai/models/nano-banana-pro)).

**Selection heuristic (what we ship).**
1. **Cap at 2 reference images per request.** More = slower + dilutes the channel-specific composition prompt.
2. **Semantic match first.** If the user request mentions "product launch" / "team photo" / a specific feature, pick the asset whose filename or alt-text best matches. (For v1 we don't have alt text — fall back to filename keyword overlap; if no match, fall back to recency.)
3. **Recency fallback.** Most recently uploaded asset wins ties.
4. **Skip oversized.** Skip any asset >5 MB (preserve current guard) — Gemini accepts up to 7 MB but our local-storage transit gets flaky over 5.

**What this unlocks.** The model uses the reference for *colour-grade*, *lighting*, *product-fidelity*. The text prompt drives composition, aspect, and channel idiom. Together this stops generic-AI-stock-photo output and produces "looks like the brand shot it" output.

## 6. Stripping web-UI leakage from image prompts

**The bug.** Today, `image_gen._build_prompt` would (or did) inject `style_profile.generation_guidance` into the image prompt. That field is captured by Gemini-vision for **website rendering** — it contains instructions like "use 980 px corner radius for primary buttons", "use Inter for body text", "16 px grid". Those instructions bleed into the image as **rendered UI artefacts**: rounded button pills, fake nav bars, "GET STARTED" CTA chips. Classic AI-slop tells ([colecandoo, 2026](https://colecandoo.com/2026/02/07/stop-making-ai-slop-for-social-media-clout/)).

**Fix — explicit field allow/deny lists.**

**SAFE for image prompts (allow):**
- `style_profile.mood`
- `style_profile.composition`
- `style_profile.photographic_vs_illustrated`
- `style_profile.distinctive_marks` (top 3)
- `palette` (top 4) + `palette_roles` (bg/primary/text)
- `identity.typography.body` *only* if the channel's image carries text (TikTok cover, LinkedIn banner with overlay) — never for IG feed, blog header, X.

**UI-only — DO NOT inject (deny):**
- `style_profile.generation_guidance` ← the buggy one
- `identity.radii` (button corner radii — only for web rendering)
- `identity.shadows`
- `identity.button_tokens`
- `identity.components` (any web component spec)

**Anti-prompt section (explicit negative list).** Append to every image prompt:

> *Critical: this is social-media feed content, not a website screenshot. Do NOT include UI elements (buttons, navigation bars, dropdowns, search bars, form inputs, sliders, modal dialogs, scroll indicators, browser chrome). Do NOT render text as a CTA pill. Do NOT use rainbow gradients, shiny chrome 3D, generic AI-stock-photo aesthetics, stock corporate handshakes, fake-creator AI faces, or watermarks. The image should look like an in-house brand photographer/designer made it for this specific channel.*

This negative block is the single highest-leverage fix for the leakage bug. ([LTX Studio negative prompts](https://ltx.studio/blog/negative-prompts); [getimg.ai negative prompts](https://getimg.ai/guides/guide-to-negative-prompts-in-stable-diffusion) — focused negative lists outperform long ones; this one is targeted at the actual failure modes we see.)

## 8. Concrete file-by-file implementation plan

### 8.1 `app/services/agent/channels.py`

Extend `Channel` dataclass with research-grounded fields; add the 5 new channels (story, tiktok, youtube, newsletter, carousel). Schema:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Channel:
    id: str                       # "linkedin" | "instagram" | "instagram_story" | "x" | "tiktok" | "blog" | "newsletter" | "youtube"
    label: str                    # "LinkedIn" — used in UI badge
    glyph: str                    # "in" | "ig" | "x" | "tt" | "yt" | "✉" | "📰" — UI mark
    text_kind: Literal["post","caption","tweet","script","article","email"]
    word_min: int
    word_max: int
    image: ChannelImage | None    # aspect, aspect_label, role, target_px, gen_aspect (snap to nano-banana supported)
    tint: str                     # hex for UI tint
    schema_field: str             # JSON key in LLM response
    idiom_notes: str              # 2-3 sentences of channel-specific drafting guidance (research-grounded)
    voice_adaptation_note: str    # one sentence: how the channel modulates brand voice
    anti_patterns: list[str]      # AI-slop tells specific to this channel — fed into both text and image anti-prompts
    asset_selection_hint: str     # which kind of asset best anchors this channel's image
```

`ChannelImage` gains `target_px: tuple[int,int]` and `gen_aspect: str` (the nano-banana-supported aspect — for LinkedIn this becomes `"16:9"` even though display is 1.91:1).

`resolve_channels(brand, user_text)` keeps current logic; the **DEFAULT_CHANNELS** changes to `["linkedin", "instagram", "x", "blog"]` so the demo always emits 4 channel-coordinated artefacts.

### 8.2 `app/services/agent/prompts.py`

`channel_post_messages(brand, channel, user_request)` injects the 5-layer prompt stack (voice profile → idiom_notes → voice_adaptation_note → anti_patterns → user request). Each channel's `_system_for(channel)` consumes `channel.idiom_notes` and `channel.voice_adaptation_note` directly so prompts stay in sync with the registry.

Critical adjustments versus current prompts:
- LinkedIn: enforce hook in first 200 chars + 3–5 hashtags at end + remove the "no clichés like 'I'm excited to'" sentence (already handled via `voice_profile.dont`).
- Instagram: hashtags **in first comment** (output schema: `{caption, first_comment}`) — front-load message in first 125 chars.
- X: drop the "≤50 words" hint; enforce **≤280 chars** literally with a length validator on the response.
- Blog: keep the GEO listicle structure (quote block + stat callout) — research confirms this is the move.
- Newsletter: new — "you and me" register, ≤500 words, single primary CTA.
- TikTok: new — outputs `{hook, script, caption}`; hook ≤2 s of speech (~5–8 words).

### 8.3 `app/services/agent/image_gen.py`

Three changes:

1. **Strip UI leakage.** `_build_prompt` reads only the SAFE fields from §6 (mood, composition, photographic_vs_illustrated, top-3 distinctive_marks, palette, palette_roles). It **explicitly does not read** `generation_guidance`, `identity.radii`, `identity.shadows`, `identity.components`. Add a code comment naming the deny-list so this can't regress.

2. **Per-channel composition guidance.** Pull from `channel.image.role` + `channel.idiom_notes` (the image-specific subset). Examples:
   - LinkedIn: "Editorial banner. Restrained palette. Strong typographic hierarchy if any text. Caption-safe negative space on the left third."
   - IG feed: "Lifestyle, product-in-context. Off-centre composition. Saturated."
   - IG story: "Vertical 9:16. Focal subject in centre safe zone (1080×1390 centred in 1080×1920). Top 250 px and bottom 280 px must be visually quiet for UI overlay."
   - X: "Single statement. Generous negative space. Bold and readable at small size."
   - TikTok cover: "Big hook text in bright bold sans-serif. Single focal subject. Vertically centred so 3:4 profile-grid crop survives."
   - Blog header: "Editorial. Soft enough that an H1 reads on top. No embedded title."

3. **Asset-as-reference selection.** New helper `_select_reference_assets(brand, channel, user_request, max_refs=2)` implementing the §5 heuristic (semantic match → recency fallback → ≤5 MB guard). Calls existing `_load_asset_bytes`. Image API call sets `image_config={"aspect_ratio": channel.image.gen_aspect}`.

4. **Append the universal anti-prompt block** from §6 verbatim.

### 8.4 `app/services/agent/loop.py`

Replace the hard-coded `linkedin + blog + hero` flow with a channel iteration:

```python
channels = resolve_channels(brand_data, text)
drafts: dict[str, dict] = {}
for ch in channels:
    await _step(campaign_id, f"drafting {ch.label} {ch.text_kind}")
    text_draft = await _safe_chat_json(
        channel_post_messages(brand_data, ch, text), schema_for(ch)
    )
    title, body = extract_text(ch, text_draft)
    drafts[ch.id] = {"title": title, "body": body}
    bus.emit(Events.DRAFT_CREATED, {
        "campaign_id": campaign_id,
        "channel": ch.id, "channel_label": ch.label,
        "title": title or ch.label,
        "preview": body[:240], "body": body,
    })

    if ch.image is not None:
        await _step(campaign_id, f"generating {ch.label} {ch.image.role}")
        img_url = await _try_channel_image(campaign_id, brand_data, ch, text)
        if img_url:
            bus.emit(Events.DRAFT_CREATED, {
                "campaign_id": campaign_id,
                "channel": f"{ch.id}_image", "channel_label": ch.label,
                "title": f"{ch.label} {ch.image.role}",
                "preview": img_url, "body": img_url,
                "image_aspect": ch.image.aspect,
            })
```

Bundle structure becomes `{ "channels": { "linkedin": {"text": ..., "image_url": ...}, ... } }` instead of the old top-level `blog` / `social.linkedin` / `hero_image_url`.

## 9. What we ship for the demo

**The smallest version that demonstrates the value.**

Four channels, four channel-appropriate images, all from one user sentence. **Default set: LinkedIn, Instagram (feed), X, Blog.**

The demo moment: the user types *"announcing our new pricing — simpler tiers, free trial extended to 30 days"* and within 30–45 seconds gets:

- **LinkedIn** — 180-word post in their voice, hook above the fold, 4 hashtags at end + a 1.91:1 banner (1600×900 generated, 16:9 source) with restrained palette, editorial composition.
- **Instagram** — 80-word caption, line breaks, hashtags in first comment + a 1:1 1080×1080 lifestyle-style square in saturated brand palette.
- **X** — a single 240-char tweet, no hashtags + a 16:9 1200×675 punchy single-statement image.
- **Blog** — 400-word listicle with quote block + stat callout + a 16:9 1600×900 editorial header.

All four read as the same brand (palette + voice + distinctive_marks consistent). All four read as native to their channel (aspect, density, register all different). All four pass the eyeball test for "human-made by an in-house team", not "AI-generated slop".

**Demo line.** *"I typed one sentence and got a full coordinated campaign — LinkedIn banner + post, IG square + caption, X image + tweet, blog header + article — all in MY brand's voice and visual style. No two assets are the same image cropped differently. Each one was made for the channel it lives on."*

**Cuttable for time:**
- TikTok / Story / Newsletter / YouTube live behind the registry but are not on the default channel set for the demo. They light up if the user's request explicitly mentions them.
- Carousels behind a feature flag (PDF generation pipeline).
- Re-cropping LinkedIn 16:9 → 1.91:1 in post-processing — ship the 16:9 if time-pressed; nobody at the demo will measure pixels.

## 10. Risks + open questions

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation / graceful degradation |
|---|---|---|
| **Image gen quota exhausted mid-demo** | Med | Per-campaign cap = 4 images. Cache last successful generation per (brand, channel, prompt-hash). Fall back to a brand-palette gradient placeholder + label "image generation rate-limited — retry" rather than crash. |
| **Asset library empty (new brand, no upload)** | High for fresh demos | Prompt works without reference assets — prompt becomes "look like the brand's palette + style_profile, photographic"; quality is lower but acceptable. Don't fail. |
| **Multi-image input limit hit / Nano Banana Pro rejects refs** | Low | Cap refs at 2 (well below the 14 limit). Catch and retry without refs on first failure. Log silently. |
| **Latency: 4 channels × 2 LLM calls (text + image) sequential = ~30–45 s** | High at demo | Run channels in parallel via `asyncio.gather`. Stream `DRAFT_CREATED` events as each finishes — UI fills in progressively, perceived latency drops. |
| **User request is vague ("post something")** | Med | Pull most recent published-channel topic from brand snapshot OR fall back to "introduce yourself" template. Either way, never block on missing input. |
| **Nano Banana Pro doesn't support 1.91:1** | Confirmed | Generate 16:9, optionally centre-crop in post (Pillow). For demo: ship 16:9. |
| **Image still has UI artefacts despite the negative prompt** | Med | Layer 2 of defence: post-gen vision-LLM check ("does this image contain a CTA button, a nav bar, or rendered UI text?"). If yes, retry once with strengthened negative prompt. Out of scope for hackathon — note as v2. |
| **Voice profile not yet captured for the brand** | Low (onboarding gates this) | Drafts gracefully degrade to neutral professional voice with explicit "voice profile not yet captured — generic draft" indicator in UI. |
| **First comment posting on IG isn't a real post mechanism in our app** | Low | We render the `first_comment` field as a separate card under the IG caption ("Post this as the first comment ↓"), human copies it. |
| **Demo brand has weird palette (e.g. all neon)** | Low | Palette role tags (`bg`, `primary`, `text`) constrain how colours are used; even neon palettes get a structured role assignment. |

**Open questions for engineering review:**
1. Do we need to actually crop the LinkedIn 16:9 → 1.91:1, or is the small extra height acceptable for the demo? (Recommend: ship 16:9, crop in v2.)
2. Should `IG carousel` be a default channel or stay opt-in? (Recommend: opt-in for v1; carousel-PDF generation is its own subsystem.)
3. Where does the post-gen "is this AI-slop?" vision check live? (Recommend: out of scope for hackathon, design for it in v2.)
4. Is the Newsletter draft a separate channel or just a downstream re-format of the Blog? (Recommend: separate — register and length differ enough to merit its own prompt.)
